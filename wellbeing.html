Artist wellbeing

WELLBEING IS PART OF THE TOUR PLAN.

Touring can be rewarding and demanding. TM360 treats mental health and wellbeing as practical planning considerations, not an afterthought.

Working towards partnership with Tonic Music

Tonic Music for Mental Health says it provides tailored mental-health support for established and emerging music artists, music-industry professionals and music fans. Its Tonic Rider programme is designed for people professionally engaged in music.

Steve Hazelden is undertaking Mental Health First Aider training through Tonic Rider. TM360 is exploring a formal partnership with Tonic Music, subject to agreement. Until confirmed, TM360 does not describe itself as an official Tonic partner.

Tonic Rider support includes

  • Mental Health First Aid training
  • Suicide First Aid Lite
  • Peer-support groups
  • Music performance anxiety workshops
  • Emotional resilience and ACT skills

The TM360 wellbeing commitment

Plan responsibly

Consider routing, travel, accommodation, load-in requirements, breaks, meals, access needs and cumulative pressure before commitments are fixed.

Communicate openly

Set clear expectations, create appropriate check-in opportunities, respect boundaries and discuss practical concerns early.

Know the limits

Listen without judgement, respond calmly and signpost qualified help. TM360 does not diagnose or provide therapy.

Before, during and after the tour

Before

  • Access and adjustment needs
  • Rest and sleep considerations
  • Communication preferences
  • Known pressure points
  • Emergency contacts

During

  • Proportionate wellbeing check-ins
  • Review pressure from routing and timings
  • Resolve practical problems early
  • Support respectful communication
  • Signpost specialist help where appropriate

After

  • Review avoidable pressure
  • Assess routing and recovery
  • Capture lessons for future tours
  • Agree practical improvements
  • Signpost continuing support if needed

Supporting touring realities

  • Performance anxiety
  • Emotional resilience
  • Late nights and disrupted sleep
  • Financial insecurity
  • Interpersonal pressure
  • Substance-use awareness

What TM360 does not provide

  • Therapy or counselling
  • Diagnosis or medical advice
  • Psychiatric care
  • Clinical crisis intervention
  • A replacement for professional healthcare

Urgent support

Tonic Music states that it is not a crisis service and directs people requiring urgent help to NHS 111. If there is an immediate risk to life, call 999 or attend A&E.
